# Entrepreneurs — physical playtest feedback

Every paper feedback form from a face-to-face session, transcribed. The forms
themselves are photographs; this is the only searchable copy, and it exists
because evidence that lives in a camera roll is evidence nobody can check.

Scale is 1–5. The form labels it "1 = Poor/Too Little, 5 = Excellent/Too Much",
which makes **Game Length ambiguous** — some players read it as a quality score
and some as "how long did it feel", and several struck the Too Little/Too Much
wording out. That column is reported per player and deliberately **not**
averaged.

Quoted text is verbatim, including the swearing and the spelling. Where a word
on the page is genuinely unclear it is marked `[?]`; nothing is tidied, and
nothing is paraphrased into the quotes. Portuguese answers are given as written,
with a translation beside them.

## What has been done about each

**The seat you are dealt — now named by four players across three versions.**
Amy wanted "every starter player gets same resources + money". Rick wanted the
starting money reversed. Hanna says "the last player is kinda set to lose" and
wants a Catan-style snake order. Chris, at the same table, says there "was no
challenge to Player 1". That is four independent reports pointing the same way.

`audit_seat.js` was re-run over 1000 games a table size against the current
game, and the answer is **not the one this file carried a week ago**. The old
reading — seat 1 worst, seat 3 best, equal capital and cards the only fix — was
measured before v21 and does not survive it.

What the current game does:

| | seat 1 | seat 2 | seat 3 | seat 4 | spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 players | 28.7% | **41.9%** | 29.3% | — | 13.2 |
| 4 players | 26.0% | **29.5%** | 23.0% | 21.5% | 8.0 |

**Seat 2 is the best seat and both ends of the table pay for it.** At three
players seats 1 and 3 land within half a point of each other, well under the
33.3% baseline, and the middle runs away with it. The compensation curve is not
too steep or too shallow — it is the wrong shape.

**And at one year, Hanna is simply right.** `--len` was added to the probe to cut
the game to the length her table played rather than argue with her. At four
players the last seat wins **17.5% against a 25.0% baseline** over four quarters,
a 16.8-point spread; 24.1% over eight; 21.5% over twelve. A Blueprint is worth
what you have time to build and run, so the late seat's three cards need a long
game to pay, while capital and delivery order pay from quarter one. The
compensation is sized for twelve quarters and is wrong at four.

**Rick's wand now measures as a good change.** He asked for the starting money
to be reversed; the pre-v21 probe said that made it worse, and on the current
game it is one of the two arms that close the gap — four players to a 4.4-point
spread from 8.0, three players to 8.8 from 13.2. Rotating turn order, which is
close to what Hanna asked for, takes three players to 7.0. The arm that used to
be the fix, equal capital and equal cards, is now among the worst: it opens the
four-player spread to 9.3 and makes seat 1 dominant at three. v21 pays for
building early and wide, so capital compounds where it used to sit, and giving
the most of it to the seat that also moves first stacks two advantages that now
multiply.

Nothing has been applied. The shape of the fix now depends on game length, which
is a bigger decision than picking a column of numbers: a one-year teaching game
and a twelve-quarter game want opposite openings.

What the simulation enforces but the bots do not fight over is **delivery
order**. Turn order decides who sells first and demand icons are first come
first served; bots build without asking who will reach an icon before them. A
table that says player 1 ran away is pointing at that, and it is the one part of
seat 1's privilege the numbers above cannot price.

**The Megacorp reads as a punishment — Fábio and Abner, same table, independently.**
"Frustrado com perder pontos com mega corporações" and "When I did my megacorp,
I losted all my resources, without a good reward", with Abner listing megacorp
under *underpowered*. This is the first consistent negative on the endgame, and
it reverses Rick's earlier form, where "MEGA CORP" was the single most satisfying
moment of his game. The difference is version: Rick played before v21. Not yet
measured against the current cards.

**A Persona that is never used — Abner, with the designer's confirmation.**
Fábio won the 25/10 table holding the Resort Developer and did not use the
ability once. The six Personas are not the same shape: four act at Revenue, every
quarter there is something to sell (White-Label Supplier, Supply Chain Expert,
Concession Holder, Public Health Director), while two act only at the moment
their holder upgrades their own industry, and only to change the direction of
that upgrade (Systems Architect, Resort Developer). A power that waits for an
upgrade that may never come is a power a player can win without noticing, which
is what happened. Recorded; not yet measured.

**Theme fell from 4.7 to 3.8 on v21, and Abner wrote the reason on the form:
"miss flavor texts".** The cards carry a name, an image, and numbers, and no
voice. The rulebook's own position is that the jargon is load-bearing rather
than decorative, which is a defence of the *terms* and not an argument against
sixty lines of flavour. Cheap to add, and it is the only named cause for the
one score that moved the wrong way.

**Rules clarity rose from 3.1 to 3.6 on v21** — four of five at 3 or 4, against
four of seven at 2 or 3 before. `TEACH.md` and the beginner game were written
against exactly this. It is not solved: Hanna's "learning this from reading a
book of rules would probably mean no playing for me" is a direct hit on the
rulebook as a teaching instrument, and Chris's "felt like I was playing a
different game than the rest" is what a half-taught table feels like from
inside.

**Hanna's beginner suggestions overlap the beginner game that already exists**
(8 quarters, one action a track, no Megacorps) and extend it in two ways it does
not cover: starting with a company already on the board, and simplified
components rather than simplified rules. Both are worth testing.

**Land awards read as overpowered to the player they were built for.** Fábio
marks "+8 points from land and districts" as the overpowered thing, with "valor
do aluguel" beneath it. Those awards are the v21 change that pays horizontal
industries for spreading, and they are deliberately large — `4 + player count`
to the outright leader, twice a year. A player naming them as the strongest
thing on the board is the intended effect being felt, but it is also the first
report that they may be felt as *too* strong.

**Amber's "I couldn't sell my products due to market limitations"** remains the
clearest player-side report of production that finds no demand icon. Retail and
Manufacturing still dump over half their output at $1, which is intended for
them — but a player who builds *up* and cannot sell is reading the same wall
from the inside.

**"Assistant" hire overpowered — Nikola and Flora, independently.** A v2.0
mechanic that predates the current Personas; recorded because two players
flagged the same card without conferring.

**Game length.** Rick's suggestion of a special fourth quarter carrying an event
card that alters the market is close to the **Events** expansion already drafted
on the sell sheet. Arthur's "game was bit too long" is about a 300-minute
2.5-year table, which is the honest number to compare against the box's 120–180.

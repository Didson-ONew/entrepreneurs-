How to print

  • A4, 100% scale, no fit-to-page. On US Letter print at 94%. Everything sits inside a 10 mm margin.
  • Cards and tiles on 200–250 gsm card; boards and aids on plain paper or 120 gsm.
  • Cut on the printed outlines. Nothing bleeds, nothing needs a die.

Seats are drawn at random. Blueprints are drafted in reverse seat order - the last seat picks first - from the face-up top cards of the six industry decks, which are shuffled whole.

On the table

  • The four centre tiles shuffled into the middle 2×2; twelve of the 16 suburb tiles drawn for the ring around them.
  • Price markers on the base prices. Every pot empty.
  • 12 discs and 2 workers each (3 each at two players); one persona each, face up.
  • Megacorp tiles: two from each tier in play - tiers 4 and 3 always, tier 2 from 3 players, tier 1 from 4; three per tier at five players, all sixteen at six.
  • Working tracks: 4 slots each at two to four players, 5 at five, 6 at six. Board Meeting: 2 seats, the second sealed until the IPO tile is claimed.
  • The game is 8 quarters: two years. The three-year variant plays 12.

The draft, then the first quarter

  • Last seat picks first, one face-up top card at a time, until every seat has its count.
  • Planning starts with the first seat. Two workers each, three at two players.
  • Every company is paid at the Closing of the quarter it is built in - there is no waiting for a delivery.

Road prices

A plot's road price is the number on the road point it touches: a corner plot (NW, NE, SW, SE) reads its district's corner, an edge plot (N, E, S, W) reads the middle of that edge. The price is fixed by position, so the mat is printed once; lay the sixteen tiles on it, or beside it.

A plot costs its road price + $3 for every structure standing on it or orthogonally touching it, and sells for the same. The centre is dear because the four centre districts meet there.

Prices run 1 on the outer edge to 6 in the dead centre, with 3s and 5s on the middles of the roads between districts.

PLAYER AID · a quarter in three phases

1 · Planning

Place your workers one at a time, in turn order. Early is more actions, late is better information.

2 · Action

Each track resolves right to left: the last worker placed acts first and takes 1 action + 1 per worker placed after it. Board Meeting takes both your workers for one action.

3 · Closing nobody is handed the board

  1. Persona choices (concession, supply chain)
  2. Bills: $2 a level to each landlord, the rest into the suppliers' pots
  3. Count · band · multiply - every company at once
  4. HQs bank price ÷ tier; 1 EP tithe to each rival beside them
  5. Pots split evenly among each industry's companies
  6. 1st player places a hub on an empty unowned plot
  7. Year end: land awards, loan buy-back

Count the icons you reach

  • Your industry's rows, columns up to your level, in every district you reach. Rows 1 and 4 are the same industry: two icons a column there.
  • You reach the districts your plots stand in. A plot beside a hub puts you on the whole hub network; a Megacorp HQ counts as a hub.
  • An icon is never used up - everyone who reaches it counts it.
  • Read the band on your card: under the first threshold $, from it $$, from the second $$$. Multiply the price under your industry's marker.

Industry abilities

  • UT counts a block of districts as wide as its level around it; never uses hubs
  • RE a chain is its level in shopfronts: a branch per level above the first, each on owned ground in a district it reaches; never uses hubs
  • HO counts each business or hub within its level in plots as an icon, on top of the grid
  • MA counts up to its level in icons from the other rows of its own district, on top of its own
  • HC reaches every district on the hub network without touching a hub
  • TE its band row is its own: the doubler is built into its multipliers

Each industry pays three others and is paid by three. Building moves your own price down $1 and each supplier on the card up $1, on a $2–$12 track with hard ends.

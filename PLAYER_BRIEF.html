Entrepreneurs · Rulebook v13 · tabletop · three-minute briefing

You are building a city’s economy from nothing.

Buy land. Build companies on it. Sell what they make to the neighbourhoods around them. Most Entrepreneurial Points after three years wins — twelve quarters, and then it is over.

The one idea underneath everything

Every company pays running costs each quarter, and that money does not vanish. It goes to the industries printed on its Blueprint as suppliers. So the industry nobody is building is quietly collecting everyone else’s money — its price climbs while the crowded ones sink toward $1.

If you remember one thing at the table: watch what nobody is building.

A quarter, in five steps

You only make decisions in the first two. The rest resolves around you.

  1. Planning — place your two workers on the four action tracks.
  2. Action — the tracks resolve and each worker takes its actions.
  3. Production — every company pays its OPEX. Rent goes to whoever owns the ground; the rest flows into the supplier pots.
  4. Revenue — you deliver what you produced to demand icons for cash, then the pots are shared out.
  5. Closing — a new Logistic Hub is built on an empty plot. At the end of Q4, Q8 and Q12 the two land awards are paid.

Three things worth holding on to

Last in, first out

Workers fill a track left to right, but the track resolves right to left. Whoever places last acts first. Going early buys you extra actions — one for every worker that lands after you — but everyone else acts before you, and may take the exact plot or card you were waiting for.

Companies pay the moment they open

Build a company and it scores 2 EP per level immediately, straight into your bank — no waiting, nothing held back on the card. Upgrade it and it scores again at its new level, on the spot. Building is not how you fund your score; building is the score.

Your ten discs

One disc on every plot you own, one on every company you run, one in the bank for every loan you have not repaid. With no free disc you cannot buy, build or borrow — however much cash you are holding. You also have only five company slots for the whole game.

Seven things worth knowing before turn one

  • The last seat drafts first. Later seats start with less money and more Blueprints — reverse order is the only catch-up in the game.
  • You each have a persona, face up from the start — a small power on one industry that nobody else has. Read yours, and everyone else’s, before you draft.
  • The decks are shuffled whole. A level 3 company can be sitting on top from the very first pick — worth taking a loan for, if you dare.
  • Hubs stand on plots. Each one fills that plot for good and puts its own district on the network. Your company joins by standing edge-to-edge with it — up to four plots can.
  • The demand grid opens up over time. Rows 3 and 4 of every district stay closed until Q5, and the whole grid is wiped clean after Q8.
  • Each demand icon can be sold to once, by whoever reaches it first. Anything you cannot sell is recycled for $1 a unit.
  • You may build on someone else’s land. Rent is $3 for every level standing on a plot, paid to whoever owns that plot — so a two-storey corner pays its landlord $6 while the neighbour takes $3. The company is still yours.
  • Selling a company does not destroy it. The building stays standing as a Distressed Asset, and anyone can take it over — for half its setup as it stands, or rebuilt with a new Blueprint. Including you, later, when you can afford it again.

A horizontal company takes one plot per level and those plots must share edges — corners do not count. A vertical one stays on the plot it started on, whatever its level.

Don’t overthink turn one: buy a plot or build your cheapest Blueprint and watch what everyone else takes. Build early and build often — every company pays you the day it opens, and again the day it grows. And nobody at this table has played before either.
